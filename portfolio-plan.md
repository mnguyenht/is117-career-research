## Two approaches

Both serve the same reader and need, and use the same hero copy, projects and links. They differ in **order** and **visual treatment**.

| | A. Proof first | **B. Work index (chosen)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idea | Lead with one case study, the way an FDE would pitch: problem, constraints, what I built, result | Lead with the work itself: a short index of my strongest projects, each with one line and its proof |
| First screen | Hero copy, then my client site as a featured case study with a "Read the case study" action | Hero copy, then the first entries of the Selected Work index |
| Sections | Hero → featured case study → T-shirt and group case studies → other work → materials and contact | Hero → Selected Work (6 entries) → Also (one-line list) → materials and contact. Case studies one click deeper |
| Main action | Read the featured case study | Open a project's proof or case study |
| Visual direction | Modernist: one typeface, strict grid, black on white, one accent colour for the main action. Reference: Swiss International Style, function and hierarchy first | Editorial and layered: mixed type sizes, overlapping thumbnails, timeline marks borrowed from video editing. Reference: postmodern layering |
| Fits | Shows one scoping story in depth | Shows range and recent momentum at a glance, which my CV already has |
| Risk | Visitors who want range may not scroll past the first case study | A grid shows _what_ I made but not _why_, the flaw I noted in [Rauno's Craft page](https://mnguyenht.github.io/is117-career-research/#portfolio-references) |
| Portfolio reference | [Amandeep Mander](https://amandeep-mander.pages.dev/): case-study fields | [Rauno Freiberg](https://rauno.me/craft): dated index |

**My choice: B.** In my words: "I already have a hefty CV with all my works, would be easy to just list out all the impressive stuff first hand."

**Handling B's risk:** every index entry says who it was for and what shipped, not only its name, and the case studies use Mander's fields so the _why_ is one click away.

**What the comparison can show:** which order helps a first-time visitor pass story S1 faster. Because order and visuals change together, it cannot say which one caused a difference.

## User stories

Each check is something a classmate can do and see pass or fail.

| ID | Story | Essential feature | Observable check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | As an FDE hiring manager following my résumé link, I want to see the role I'm aiming for and my strongest work right away, so I can decide whether to keep reading. | Hero with name, school, year and target, followed directly by Selected Work | A classmate who hasn't seen the site names my target role and opens the CorporateConnections entry within 60 seconds. At 1280 × 800 the hero and the first three entries show without scrolling; at 375 px wide the first entry starts within one screen. The hero is real text, not an image. |
| S2 | As that manager, I want every index entry to tell me what it was and let me check it, so I don't have to trust a list. | Each entry: date, label (Client, Work, Open source, Course project or Competition), one line on who it was for and what shipped, and a proof link | All six Selected Work entries show all four parts, and every proof link opens (no 404s, checked with a link checker). No unlinked number appears anywhere on the site. |
| S3 | As a reviewer, I want case studies for the T-shirt and group projects that show my choices, my part and how I checked the work. | Case-study pages with fixed headings: Who it's for · Options compared · What I built · AI use and what I checked · Feedback that changed it · Links | Both pages show all six headings. The T-shirt page links the [live store](https://standard-issue-is117.vercel.app), its [repo](https://github.com/mnguyenht/is117-brand-assignment) and at least one issue or commit where a choice was made. The group page names the [shared repo](https://github.com/JivExperimenters/group-business), every member with their GitHub username, and at least one commit or PR I authored. |
| S4 | As a recruiter, I want my GitHub, LinkedIn and résumé one click away from every page. | Header links to all three; résumé as a one-page PDF | On every page, all three links exist and open the real profiles in a signed-out browser. The résumé is one page, its text can be selected and copied, and its project names and dates match the site. |
| S5 | As someone who wants to contact Minh or read the site's code, I want a professional contact and the source. | Footer with an email link to qn22@njit.edu and a link to this site's repo | The email link opens a new message addressed to qn22@njit.edu, and the repo link opens. No phone number or home address appears on the site or in the public résumé. |

**Promise to feature:** "with real people in mind" becomes the _Who it's for_ heading in every case study and the "who it was for" line on every index entry (S2, S3). No hiring or traffic result is claimed.
